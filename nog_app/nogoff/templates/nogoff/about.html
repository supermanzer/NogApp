Ode to the Nog Off

When winter lays its heavy hand
across the world in grey and white,
and darkness claims the shortened day
and steals the last of fading light —

We gather close, we brave the cold,
we stir the cream and crack the eggs,
we grate the nutmeg, pour the spirit,
raise our glasses, bend our legs —

For this is not mere drinking, friend,
this is tradition, rite, and lore:
the ancient, noble Nog Off calls,
and none who hears it can ignore.

Each nog a testament to craft,
to warmth conjured from simple things —
the richness of a careful pour,
the joy a humble cupful brings.

The darkness presses at the glass,
the cold wind rattles at the door,
but here inside the nog burns bright,
a golden bulwark to the core.

And when the votes are tallied up,
when all have sipped and all have scored,
one nog shall rise above the rest —
one maker reap their just reward.

For they who craft the finest nog,
who wrest from winter's grip some cheer,
shall wear the crown above all others
and reign supreme throughout the year:

The Neptune of Nog.

So raise your cup to those who dare
to push the humble nog to art,
who hold the darkness back each year
with nutmeg, spirit, cream, and heart.

Long live the Nog Off. Long may it stand
against the cold and sunless months.
May every winter find us here,
and may it never come but once.
